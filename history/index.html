IFTS / History & scale

From a founding assembly of 31 to a 41-country forum

The Forum was founded in 2012 in Prague. Thirteen editions later it convenes more than three hundred delegates each year from research, industry, operator organisations and transport authorities.

Founding

The International Forum on Transport Standards was established at a founding assembly held in Prague on 19-20 September 2012. The assembly was convened by transport research groups and industry practitioners from 9 countries and adopted the Forum Charter, which defines IFTS as an independent, not-for-profit forum organisation dedicated to comparing and improving the practice of transport measurement, ranking and reporting.

The first full edition took place in Prague in June 2013 with 122 registered delegates from 14 countries. The Forum has met every year since, rotating among European host cities; the 2020 and 2021 editions were held online.

Evolution of topics

2013-2016

Infrastructure and data foundations

Early editions focused on interoperability: common vocabularies for multimodal information, reference data architectures for mobility services, and the harmonisation of service quality indicators across modes.

2017-2020

Digital services and open data

The Forum turned to data governance: freight data exchange practice, benchmarking of transit performance, open data in corridor management, and - from 2020 - resilience indicators for disrupted networks.

2021-2023

Measurement and scenario practice

Editions 2021-2023 addressed measurement under uncertainty: remote operations data, model quality assurance in demand forecasting, and scenario frameworks for infrastructure evaluation.

2024-2025

Ranking and reporting standards

The current cycle focuses on how performance rankings are produced and reported: comparability of reporting (2024) and the stability of scenario-based rankings and the reporting standards that govern them (2025).

04 /

Structure and outputs

Governance

The Forum is led by a Forum Chair elected by the participants for a term of three years. The Programme Council sets the annual theme and reviews the keynote programme. The Secretariat, based in Prague, organises the editions and maintains the archive. Four standing working groups carry the technical programme between editions.

Outputs

Each edition concludes with Forum Conclusions and the annual IFTS Position Paper. Position Papers record the state of practice discussed during the edition and the Forum's recommendations for voluntary adoption. IFTS does not issue standards and is not a standardisation body.
